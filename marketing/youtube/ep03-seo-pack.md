## 5. Channel SEO strategy: AI Field Notes

### 5.1 Main idea: own the "private AI on your own laptop" cluster first

The broad terms (`ai tools` at 666K searches, competition 70; `local ai` at 373K, competition 70) can't be won by a 1-subscriber channel. The Ollama and local-AI cluster has three things a new channel needs:

- **High demand:** `ollama tutorial` 123K, `local llm` 77K, `how to use ollama` 47.6K.
- **Winnable gaps:** `lm studio vs ollama` (30.8K, 25), `what is ollama` (20.1K, 27), `ollama install windows` (13.3K, 28), `is local ai safe` (21.3K, 37).
- **A fit with the brand:** privacy for client work is a real sole-trader problem, and most Ollama tutorials are made for developers.

Build one playlist, **Run AI Privately**, into a set of 5–6 linked videos, so each one sends viewers on to the next:

| Order | Video | Target keyword | Searches / Comp. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hub | Ep 3: Ollama Tutorial | ollama tutorial | 123K / 46 |
| 2 | Ep 5: LM Studio Tutorial for Beginners | lm studio tutorial | 28K / 38 (15 Nov) |
| 3 | **LM Studio vs Ollama: Which Should a Beginner Use?** (new) | lm studio vs ollama | 30.8K / 25 |
| 4 | Ep 8: Can a Free Local AI Replace ChatGPT for Small Business Work? | local llm | 77K / 48 |
| 5 | **Is Local AI Safe? What Stays Private and What Doesn't** (new; also fits the AI Safety pillar) | is local ai safe | 21.3K / 37 |
| 6 | **Use Claude Code With Ollama** (new, optional) ³ | ollama claude code | 202K / 32 |

³ This has the most searches of any term in the cluster, but it's for developers, not your core audience. Before you script it, check the current Ollama and Anthropic docs to confirm the integration works and how. Don't rely on memory or third-party posts. Treat it as a reach video, not a core one.

**Recommended order:** Ep 3, then **LM Studio vs Ollama** (move it before Ep 4; it's the easiest high-volume gap in the data), then Ep 5, then Ep 8, then Is Local AI Safe.

### 5.2 On-page rules for every upload

1. **Title:** put the keyword in the first 40 characters, keep the whole title 50–65 characters, and only promise what the video shows.
2. **Description:** put the keyword and the result in the first two lines. Then setup and versions, chapters with keyword-rich labels, commands and links, a "watch next" link, the disclosure, and three hashtags.
3. **Say it out loud:** say the main keyword in the first 15 seconds. YouTube's automatic captions are indexed.
4. **Captions:** check the auto-captions for technical names (Ollama, LM Studio, model names) before publishing.
5. **Thumbnail:** follow the kit: your real face, one object, at most 3 words, and no company logos. For Ep 3, use "NO WI-FI?" with a crossed-out Wi-Fi icon. The current draft scored 22/100, so redo it before upload.
6. **End screen and cards:** point to the next video in the playlist, not just "subscribe".
7. **Pinned comment:** ask a question that brings useful replies (specs, models, corrections).

### 5.3 Shorts as the top of the funnel

Cut 2–3 Shorts from each long video, each linking to the full video through YouTube's *Related video* field:

- "My Wi-Fi is off and this AI still works" (the Ep 3 cold open)
- "How much RAM do you need for local AI?" (the 1:30 chapter)
- "Read this before using a free AI model for client work" (licences)

Keep the factory cadence of one Short a day. Make at least two of every seven Shorts point to the current long video.

### 5.4 Timing

- Publish long videos on the same day and time each week. Start 2–3 hours before your audience's evening peak, then adjust once *Audience → When your viewers are on YouTube* has data. That takes roughly 1,000 views.
- Search-driven tutorials like this one build views over months, not days. Judge Ep 3 at 28 and 90 days, not at 48 hours.
- Check the rising data weekly (vidIQ "rising" mode, niche "local ai"). Fast-moving terms like "how to download ollama" (+90.7%) and "ollama ai" (+98.9%) are worth a Short within days.

### 5.5 What to measure

| Metric | Where | Rough target for a new channel | If it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impressions CTR from YouTube search | Analytics → Reach → Traffic source | 4–8% | Change the title or thumbnail, one at a time |
| Average % viewed (10-min tutorial) | Analytics → Engagement | 35%+ | Tighten the first 30 seconds and cut the setup talk |
| Share of views from YouTube search | Traffic sources | 40%+ for tutorials | Keyword in title or speech too weak; re-check intent |
| Views from end screens and playlists | Traffic sources | Growing month on month | Fix "watch next" links and end screens |
| Subscribers per 1,000 views | Analytics → Audience | 5+ | Make the channel promise clearer in the close |

These targets are common rules of thumb for small channels, not YouTube benchmarks. Replace them with your own averages after 5–10 videos.

| 1–2 | Ep 1 (proposals), Ep 3 (Ollama) | 7 a week | Finish the channel rename, playlists and upload defaults (open tasks in `youtube-channel-kit.md`) |
| 3–4 | Ep 2, LM Studio vs Ollama | 7 a week | First CTR check; title test on Ep 3 |
| 5–8 | Ep 5, Ep 4, Ep 8, Is Local AI Safe | 7 a week | Add end screens linking the private-AI videos together; update older descriptions with new links |
| 9–12 | Ep 6, Ep 7, Ep 10, one fast "New Model, Real Test" | 7 a week | 90-day review: double down on the topic with the best search share, and re-title weak videos |

### 5.7 Things to avoid

- **Title bait:** the video must deliver what the title and thumbnail promise. Viewers who leave early hurt ranking more than a modest title.
- **Stuffing the description:** write sentences, not keyword lists. YouTube's spam policies cover misleading metadata.
- **Logos and trademarks:** don't use Ollama's logo or other brand assets in thumbnails without checking their guidelines. Use a neutral llama silhouette, as the kit says.
- **Unchecked steps:** install commands and model names change. Re-check them on ollama.com on recording day, and date every description.
