## Before you record: run the test

1. Pick 5–7 real proposals or quotes you need to send this week. If a client's details are private, swap in a realistic sample brief.
2. For each one, record: the brief, the AI tool and model version, the prompt, how long the first draft took, how long editing took, what you changed, and whether you sent it.
3. Keep a "time it would normally take" estimate for each, written down **before** you start, so you aren't guessing afterwards.
4. Screen-record each session. Blur client names.
5. Note at least one proposal where the AI got something wrong, such as a price, a scope detail, or the tone.

### The prompt (1:15–2:30)

[SCREEN: the prompt]

Here's the starting prompt. It has four parts.

First, who the client is and what they asked for, in their own words where possible.
Second, what I'm offering: scope, timeline, and price. I always give the AI the price. I never let it pick one.
Third, two or three examples of proposals I've already sent, so it copies my tone instead of inventing one.
Fourth, the format: a short summary, what's included, what's not included, the timeline, the price, and the next step.

The "what's not included" line matters. That section is what stops scope creep, and the AI won't write it unless you ask.

### Who this suits (8:30–9:30)

This works best if you send similar proposals often and you already know your prices. The AI handles structure and wording. You handle the numbers and the promises.
