## Script

### Cold open (0:00–0:35)

[ON CAMERA]

If you run a business on your own, you're the boss, the worker, the receptionist, the marketing team and the bookkeeper. Most AI advice skips over that and tells you to "automate everything".

You can't hand over everything, and you shouldn't. But there are five jobs you can hand to AI this week, in a sensible order, and each one gives back time you can spend on the work that actually pays.

By the end of this video you'll have the five, the order, and the one rule that keeps AI from embarrassing you in front of a client.

### The rule first (0:35–1:30)

Here's the rule: **AI drafts, you decide.**

Anything that goes to a client — a price, a date, a promise — gets checked by you before it leaves. AI tools write fluent sentences whether the facts are right or not. A wrong number in a smooth paragraph is harder to spot than a wrong number in a messy one.

So for every job in this list, I'll show you what to hand over and what to keep.

### Job 1: replying to emails and enquiries (1:30–3:15)

Start here because it's frequent, low-risk, and you'll see the benefit by Friday.

[DEMO: paste a real enquiry, with names blurred, and ask for a reply]

Give the AI three things: the message you received, the facts of your answer in bullet points, and one or two of your past replies so it matches your voice.

The AI writes the paragraphs. You supply the facts. If you only paste the enquiry and say "reply to this", it will invent availability, prices and policies to fill the gaps.

**Hand over:** wording and structure. **Keep:** prices, dates, and anything you're committing to.

### Job 2: quotes and proposals (3:15–5:00)

This is the job most people put off, so it's worth handing over second.

[DEMO: brief → structured proposal]

Give it the client's request, your scope, your price, and an example proposal you've sent before. Ask for a set structure: a summary, what's included, what's not included, the timeline, the price, and the next step.

Ask for the "not included" section specifically. It's the part that protects you from scope creep, and AI won't add it on its own.

[I tested this for a full week in another video — link on screen.]

**Hand over:** the first draft. **Keep:** the scope decision and the price.

### Job 3: social posts and marketing (5:00–6:45)

Most sole traders know they should post more and never have time.

[DEMO: one finished job → three short posts]

Here's what works: take one real job you finished this week, and give the AI the problem, what you did, and the result. Ask for three short posts in your voice for different platforms.

Real work makes better posts than generic tips, and it's content nobody else can copy.

Watch out for anything AI adds that didn't happen, such as a "delighted customer" quote you never received. Delete it. Testimonials have to be real.

**Hand over:** turning your notes into posts. **Keep:** the facts, and approval before anything is published.

### Job 4: planning your week (6:45–8:15)

[DEMO: brain-dump list → prioritised week plan]

On Sunday night or Monday morning, dump everything in your head into one list: jobs, admin, follow-ups, personal. Ask the AI to sort it by deadline and by whether it earns money, and to suggest which day each item fits, given the hours you actually have.

It won't know that one client always pays late or that Tuesday is school pickup. Tell it, or adjust the plan yourself. The value is getting it out of your head and into an order.

**Hand over:** sorting and ordering. **Keep:** the final call on what matters.

### Job 5: follow-ups (8:15–9:30)

The job that quietly loses the most money: the quote you sent and never chased, or the invoice that's two weeks overdue.

[DEMO: list of open quotes and invoices → polite follow-up messages]

Give the AI your list of open quotes and unpaid invoices with dates, and ask for short, friendly follow-ups. Some quotes need one gentle nudge and some invoices need a firmer second message. Tell it which is which.

Then send them yourself. The AI makes it quicker to send the message you've been avoiding.

**Hand over:** the wording. **Keep:** the decision to send and the tone with each client.

### What not to hand over (9:30–10:30)

Three things stay with you.

Your prices. The AI doesn't know your costs, your market or what you're worth.

Legal and tax decisions. Use AI to understand a term or draft a question, then check with an accountant or the official source for your country.

Anything with private client details you haven't checked how the tool stores. Read the privacy settings of whatever you use, or use a tool that runs on your own computer. I've got a video on doing that for free.

### Close (10:30–11:00)

So the order is: emails, proposals, social posts, weekly planning, follow-ups. Start with one this week, not five.

AI drafts, you decide. The pages in this video got written faster, and every price, date and promise in them was still yours.
